Origin of
Genres   






    When discussing musical genres, we often focus on rhythm and instrumentation while overlooking their cultural origins. However, understanding where a sound comes from provides an important perspective for understanding why it exists. The previous visualization showed music moving through time. This map shows music moving through places.


The Electronic Migration:

    Electronic music provides one of the clearest examples. Some foundations of modern Electronic & Dance music were developed in the USA, particularly House in Chicago and Techno in Detroit. These sounds later travelled to Europe, where different regional scenes transformed them further. Germany became strongly associated with genres such as Trance, while the Netherlands became an important center for Hardstyle. What started in specific American cities therefore developed into a much wider international club and rave culture. This also helps explain the waves observed in our previous chart: Electronic music does not simply disappear and return, the genre itself continuously changes


The Rise of African and Asian Pop Cultures:

     A more recent form of musical globalization can be seen in Africa and Asia. Nigeria, with Afrobeats, and South Africa, with Amapiano and Gqom, have become important centers of contemporary African music. Streaming and social media have made it much easier for these sounds to move beyond their original markets, connecting directly with the recent growth of African Contemporary & Diaspora in our timeline. 

    Asia shows a different development. South Korea and Japan created highly organized Pop industries, while India and the Philippines adapted international influences into local forms such as Desi Hip-Hop and Pinoy R&B. The global expansion of K-Pop during the 2010s is a clear example of how a strongly localized music industry can now reach worldwide audiences.


Country and Folk: Two Different Relationships with Geography

    Not every genre follows the same path, Country remains strongly connected to the USA and even contains subgenres tied to specific areas, such as Texas Country and Red Dirt. Geography remains part of the genre's identity rather than simply its birthplace.

    Folk, instead, reflects a transatlantic tradition between the UK and North America, where songs and storytelling traditions travelled through migration and developed on both sides of the Atlantic. 

    The map therefore shows that music has always travelled. Streaming did not create musical globalization, but it dramatically increased its speed and reach.

    But location is not the only context affecting listening. Sometimes, simply changing the season changes the musical landscape.
