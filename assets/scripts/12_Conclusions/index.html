    In this Animated Beeswarm we want to find the answer to the question:
“What is the frequency of appearance for each artist?”
   Our objective is to determine how frequently artists appear in the Billboard Top 50 and whether repeated appearances have increased or decreased over time.

Results and Discussion: What the Data Actually Shows

    The animation suggests a gradual change in how chart success is distributed. Earlier periods, particularly around the late 1960s, show a larger number of different artists with relatively limited individual counts. As the animation progresses, the distribution changes: fewer unique artists appear, while the most successful artists accumulate increasingly high numbers of chart entries.
    In simple terms, the chart moves toward fewer artists with more appearances each. One possible explanation is the transformation of music consumption. During the physical era, success depended heavily on record purchases, radio and television. Digital distribution and especially streaming changed this structure.

    Listeners can now repeatedly stream the same artists without purchasing another product. More importantly, when a major artist releases an album, multiple tracks can immediately generate millions of streams at the same time. A superstar can therefore occupy several chart positions simultaneously rather than relying on a single promoted song.

    Social media and recommendation algorithms may reinforce this effect. Established artists already have enormous audiences that can immediately interact with new releases, while playlists and recommendations can maintain their visibility.
    This creates the most interesting contradiction in our story. Streaming makes more music from more countries and genres accessible than ever before, yet the Beeswarm suggests that success at the top of the charts may simultaneously become more concentrated around major artists.

    Greater access, therefore, does not necessarily mean equally distributed attention.
