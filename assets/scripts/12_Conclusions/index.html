Conclusion





    Across the five visualizations, the musical landscape from 1960 to 2025 emerges as a story of movement and transformation.

    Genres first move through time. Rock dominates much of the earlier landscape before Hip-Hop, Electronic music and other genres reshape it. Older genres do not simply disappear: Jazz, Blues, Soul and Funk leave elements that survive inside the music that follows them.

    Music also moves through geography. House develops in Chicago, Techno in Detroit and Electronic music expands into European rave culture. Folk crosses the Atlantic, while African and Asian music increasingly enters the international market. This movement existed long before the internet, but streaming and social media dramatically increased its speed and reach.

    Our seasonal analysis then shows that listening is also shaped by everyday behaviour. Winter produces a more concentrated environment, while Summer shows the greatest variety, potentially influenced by holidays, festivals and outdoor social activity.

    Finally, the artist analysis reveals a paradox: music is becoming more diverse in origin while chart success appears increasingly concentrated among major artists.

    The modern musical landscape is therefore not simply more diverse or more global than it was in 1960. It is a combination of both expansion and concentration.
    For marketing, playlist curation and artist promotion, the message is clear: popularity cannot be understood through genre alone. Time, geography, season, technology and artist visibility all interact in shaping what reaches the charts.

    Ultimately, the data does not only show what people listened to. It shows how the world around music changed, and how music changed with it.
